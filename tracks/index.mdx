A **Track** is the home for work that has continuity.

Use a Track when a subject will keep evolving over time, such as a client account, product launch, hiring effort, research project, ongoing function, or any other body of work where context and progress accumulate.

Instead of starting from scratch in every [Chat](/chats/index), a Track gives Giga and your team a durable place to keep the information that belongs to that work.

## When should you use a Track?

A Track is useful when **continuity matters**.

Create or use one when:

- the work will continue across more than one Chat
- people need a shared understanding of what has already happened
- files, decisions, or research should stay attached to the same subject
- you want Giga to retrieve the right history without re-explaining the topic every time
- progress will accumulate over days, weeks, or months

For quick one-off questions that do not need durable context, a normal [Chat](/chats/index) may be enough.

## How Tracks keep context useful

Giga separates different kinds of information instead of flattening everything into one giant history.

| Layer             | What it is for                                                                 |
| ----------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Context Notes** | Standing background and instructions that should remain true across the Track  |
| **Memory**        | Decisions, commitments, ideas, open questions, preferences, facts and procedures |
| **Files**         | Authored work product such as reports, drafts, code, documents, and data files |
| **Chats**         | The Chats where people and Giga work through tasks                             |

This separation helps Giga retrieve the right kind of context for the question being asked.

> **Files and memory are separate**
>
> An empty Track folder does not mean the Track has no history. Files are work product, while Track context and memory are stored separately.

## Tracks and Groups

Every Track lives inside a [Group](/groups/index). The Group determines the access scope the Track sits inside.

| Use a Track for                                       | Use a Group for                                    |
| ----------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| One ongoing project, area, client, or body of work    | A broader people and permission boundary           |
| Keeping subject-specific context and history together | Organizing who can access shared work              |
| Files and memory related to one subject               | Holding multiple Tracks and other shared resources |

A Track’s visibility currently follows the **Group it lives in**. Personal work should usually live in your existing private Group, while shared work belongs in an appropriate shared Group.

[Learn more about Groups →](/groups/index)

## Track context and memory

Tracks carry more than files.

Giga can use:

- **standing context** about what the Track is and how the work should be handled
- **memory**: the Track's current decisions, open commitments, ideas, open questions, preferences, facts and procedures, each with the evidence behind it
- authored files that people and Giga create over time

When you start work in a Track, Giga reads its memory first. When someone asks about the latest progress, decisions, or what changed, Giga answers from the Track's memory before assuming the answer lives in a file.

<Callout kind="info">Giga never silently attaches a Chat to a Track. Memory saved from a Chat becomes available to everyone on its Track, so you choose or confirm the Track. Anything saved without one stays private to you in **Brain → To file** until you file it.</Callout>
