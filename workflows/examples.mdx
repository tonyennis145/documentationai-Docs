Workflows are useful when a task has **several meaningful stages that should run in a consistent order**.

The examples below focus on process structure. The live Giga environment determines which Skills, Connections, files, and tools are available during a run.

## Account research brief

An **Account Research Brief Workflow** could turn company research into a consistent sales brief.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Gather account evidence" icon="search">
    Collect current company facts and relevant evidence from approved sources.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Evaluate useful signals" icon="filter">
    Identify the signals, risks, and open questions that matter for the sales team.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Prepare the brief" icon="file-text">
    Return the findings in the team’s expected structure.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Possible runtime inputs:

- company name
- company website
- research depth

An [Account Research Skill](/skills/examples) could provide the detailed research method inside the first step.

## Candidate interview brief

A **Candidate Interview Brief Workflow** could standardize how recruiting prepares for an interview.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Gather candidate evidence" icon="search">
    Review the available candidate and role material.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Evaluate against the role" icon="check-square">
    Identify relevant strengths, gaps, and evidence-backed open questions.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Prepare interview focus areas" icon="file-text">
    Return a concise brief with the areas the interviewer should probe.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Possible runtime inputs:

- candidate name
- role
- source URL

A Candidate Screening Skill can carry the reusable evaluation method while the Workflow keeps the ordered process.

## Customer issue review

A **Customer Issue Review Workflow** could give support teams a consistent diagnostic path.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Investigate the issue" icon="search">
    Gather the relevant customer context, recent activity, and available evidence.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Determine the next action" icon="git-branch">
    Identify the likely resolution or the information still required.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Prepare the response" icon="file-text">
    Draft the response or internal recommendation. Ask for confirmation before any outward-facing action.
  </Step>
</Steps>

A step can use approved [Connections](/connections/index) when the live environment makes the relevant support or customer systems available.

## Document review

A **Document Review Workflow** could separate analysis from final recommendations.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Understand the document" icon="file-text">
    Identify the purpose, audience, and important claims in the material.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Review against the standard" icon="check-circle">
    Apply the relevant review criteria and flag missing information, contradictions, or required fixes.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Prepare findings" icon="list-checks">
    Return the issues in a consistent structure, separating required changes from optional suggestions.
  </Step>
</Steps>

A reusable review [Skill](/skills/index) can hold the detailed standards used in step 2.

## Customer renewal review

A **Customer Renewal Review Workflow** could organize account context before a renewal discussion.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Gather current account context" icon="database">
    Collect the current information relevant to the renewal.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Evaluate risks and opportunities" icon="activity">
    Identify evidence-backed risks, opportunities, unresolved issues, and missing information.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Prepare the renewal brief" icon="file-text">
    Summarize the account state and the questions the team should address.
  </Step>
</Steps>

The Workflow can use live Connections when permitted, but provider access still controls which data and actions are available.

## Research report

A **Research Report Workflow** could turn a broad question into a structured piece of work.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Define the evidence needed" icon="target">
    Clarify what needs to be established and what would count as reliable support.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Gather and evaluate sources" icon="search">
    Collect relevant evidence and separate strong support from uncertainty.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Create the report" icon="file-text">
    Synthesize the findings into the required structure and clearly mark unresolved questions.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Possible runtime inputs:

- research question
- source URL
- desired depth

## Content review

A **Content Review Workflow** could standardize how marketing content moves from draft to approved recommendation.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Understand the draft" icon="file-text">
    Identify the intended audience, message, and purpose.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Review against company standards" icon="check-square">
    Apply the relevant brand, quality, or factual review method.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Prepare the revision brief" icon="edit-3">
    Return the required changes with a concise rationale.
  </Step>
</Steps>

A Brand Review Skill can carry the reusable review standard.

## Internal handoff

An **Internal Handoff Workflow** could turn active work into a concise handoff for another teammate.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Gather the current state" icon="layers">
    Collect the relevant objective, background, decisions, and current status.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Identify what matters next" icon="filter">
    Pull out unresolved questions, dependencies, risks, and immediate next steps.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Prepare the handoff" icon="users">
    Return a concise handoff with the context the next person needs to continue.
  </Step>
</Steps>

If the work belongs to a [Track](/tracks/index), the Workflow can draw from the Track context available in the current run.

## A useful Workflow pattern

Across these examples, strong Workflows tend to follow a similar shape:

| Stage | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| **Gather** | Collect the information needed for the work |
| **Evaluate** | Apply judgment, rules, or a reusable Skill |
| **Produce** | Return the useful work product or recommendation |

Real processes can have more than 3 steps, but this pattern is a good test for whether the Workflow reflects meaningful stages instead of low-level tool actions.

## Workflow, Skill, or Pulse?

Use a Workflow when the **ordered process** should be reusable.

Use a [Skill](/skills/index) when the **task method** should be reusable.

Use a [Pulse](/pulses/index) when the defining need is **recurring scheduled work**. The exact way a scheduled run uses other Giga primitives should follow the current live Pulse capabilities.
