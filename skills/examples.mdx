Skills work best when they capture a **specific repeatable method**.

The examples below show the kinds of task instructions, standards, templates, and reference context that can make a useful Skill.

## Account research

An **Account Research Skill** could teach Giga how your team researches a prospective customer.

It might define:

- which company facts to collect
- which sources or evidence to prioritize
- qualification criteria
- how to separate facts from inference
- the required output structure
- examples of a strong research summary

## Candidate screening

A **Candidate Screening Skill** could capture how your recruiting team evaluates a candidate against a role.

It might include:

- the criteria to assess
- how to distinguish evidence from assumptions
- the expected evaluation format
- interview or application evidence to reference
- examples of acceptable and weak evidence

The Skill stores the reusable screening method, while the specific candidate and role belong to the current task.

## Customer update writing

A **Customer Update Skill** could define how your team prepares recurring customer-facing updates.

It might include:

- the preferred structure
- which facts should be confirmed before drafting
- terminology to use
- tone and formatting guidance
- what should be escalated for human review

A [Chat](/chats/index) can then supply the customer-specific facts for that one update.

## Brand review

A **Brand Review Skill** could teach Giga how to review content against your company’s writing standards.

It might contain:

- brand voice rules
- words or phrases to avoid
- formatting preferences
- review criteria
- examples of acceptable and unacceptable copy

This works well as a reusable method because the same review standard can apply to many different pieces of content.

## Document review

A **Document Review Skill** could define a repeatable review checklist.

For example:

1. identify missing information
2. flag contradictions
3. check the document against the required standard
4. separate required fixes from optional suggestions
5. return findings in a consistent format

If the review becomes part of a larger ordered process, the Skill can sit alongside a [Workflow](/workflows/index).

## Meeting summary

A **Meeting Summary Skill** could define what your company wants preserved from meeting notes.

It might ask Giga to identify:

- decisions
- owners
- open questions
- follow-up actions
- dates or deadlines explicitly stated
- useful durable context

The Skill defines the extraction method. The actual meeting notes remain task input.

## Weekly reporting

A **Weekly Reporting Skill** could define how a recurring report should be prepared.

It might include:

- required sections
- how to summarize changes
- how to distinguish current state from historical detail
- metrics or evidence to include when available
- the expected final format

If the report should run automatically on a schedule, a [Pulse](/pulses/index) handles the recurring timing while the Skill can carry the reporting method where the live configuration makes it available.

## Support troubleshooting

A **Support Troubleshooting Skill** could capture a repeatable diagnostic method.

It might define:

- what evidence to collect first
- the order of diagnostic checks
- when to use a [Connection](/connections/index)
- what should be confirmed before taking an outward-facing action
- how to summarize the issue and next step

A Support Agent can carry the broader support role while the Skill stores this specific troubleshooting method.

## Handoff preparation

A **Handoff Skill** could define how Giga turns active work into a concise handoff for another teammate.

It might require:

- current objective
- relevant background
- confirmed decisions
- unresolved questions
- immediate next steps
- links or references to the relevant work

This can be useful when moving work between people without copying the entire history.

## What makes a strong Skill example?

Across these examples, the strongest Skills have the same basic properties:

| Strong Skill | Weak Skill |
| --- | --- |
| One recognizable task | Several unrelated jobs |
| Reusable method | One-off request |
| Clear standards | Vague goals |
| Useful supporting context | Large dump of unrelated information |
| Portable across relevant work | Tied unnecessarily to one specific Chat |

> **A Skill earns its place through reuse**
>
> If the instructions will only be used once, they can stay in the Chat. If the same method should guide future work, a Skill gives it a durable home.

## From example to Skill

<Steps>
  <Step title="Spot a repeated method" icon="repeat">
    Look for task instructions your team keeps explaining again.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Separate the method from the current task" icon="layers">
    Keep reusable rules in the Skill and task-specific facts in the Chat, Agent, Track, or Workflow.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Capture the method clearly" icon="file-text">
    Write the instructions, standards, output expectations, and supporting references.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Share it where useful" icon="users">
    Keep it private or share it into the Groups that should reuse it.
  </Step>
</Steps>
